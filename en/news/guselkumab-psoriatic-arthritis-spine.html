Guselkumab Eases Spinal Pain and Stiffness in Axial Psoriatic Arthritis

Share this article:

What did Johnson & Johnson announce?

On 20 November 2026, Johnson & Johnson announced that guselkumab, sold under the brand name TREMFYA, achieved MRI-confirmed improvements in spinal pain and stiffness in patients with axial psoriatic arthritis, in the Phase 4 STAR trial. The results were presented at the Emirates Society for Rheumatology Annual Meeting in Dubai, held from 20 to 22 November 2026. The trial enrolled 411 adults with active psoriatic arthritis and axial involvement confirmed by centrally-read MRI, plus elevated C-reactive protein, despite prior treatment with non-biologic disease-modifying drugs, apremilast or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and none had previously received a biologic. Participants were randomly assigned to three groups: guselkumab 100 mg every four weeks, every eight weeks, or placebo, for 24 weeks, followed by a 24-week active treatment period. The primary measure was the change in the BASDAI score, which measures disease activity from zero to ten, at Week 24. The trial met its primary endpoint and its major secondary endpoints, which is important news because axial psoriatic arthritis had, until now, no advanced therapy with MRI-proven benefit, according to the company.

Psoriatic arthritis is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the joints and skin of some people with psoriasis. Axial involvement, in the spine or sacroiliac joints, affects about 5% to 28% of patients in early disease and rises to between 25% and 70% with longer-standing disease. There is no universally accepted definition of the axial form of the disease, which makes diagnosing and studying it difficult.

What numbers were announced?

The average BASDAI score fell by 3.3 points with the every-four-weeks dose and 2.9 points with the every-eight-weeks dose, versus 2.1 points with placebo, on a scale of zero to ten. The ASDAS-CRP score, another measure of disease activity, improved by 1.5 points with the every-four-weeks dose and 1.3 points with the every-eight-weeks dose, versus 0.8 points with placebo, and both doses exceeded the 1.1-point threshold considered a clinically important improvement. MRI of the sacroiliac joints showed more than twice as much improvement with the every-four-weeks dose as with placebo: the SPARCC score, which measures inflammation on imaging, fell by 7.1 points, versus 5.2 points with the every-eight-weeks dose and only 2.4 points with placebo. High levels of skin clearance were also announced with guselkumab, without specific percentages. The HAQ-DI score, which measures the ability to perform daily activities, improved numerically more with the every-eight-weeks dose, but the difference did not reach statistical significance, so it cannot be treated as a confirmed result.

What are the limits of these results?

It is important to read these results with caution: they come from a press release by the manufacturer and a conference presentation, not yet a peer-reviewed published study, and no p-values, confidence intervals or per-arm patient numbers were released. The MRI results were also limited to the sacroiliac joints, and spinal MRI results have not yet been published. Professor Mikkel Østergaard, a paid consultant to Johnson & Johnson, said the results give doctors greater confidence in prescribing guselkumab for patients with axial psoriatic arthritis, but that statement comes from a company consultant and should be read in that context. The company's description of the drug as the «only advanced therapy» to show these improvements is a marketing claim by the company, not an independent scientific judgement. On dosing, the every-four-weeks regimen is not approved in the United States for psoriatic arthritis, where the approved regimen is every eight weeks, so if the stronger dose were adopted, it would need new regulatory approval.

What about safety?

The company said guselkumab's safety profile in the trial was consistent with its established profile from earlier studies, with no new safety signals, and no adverse-event rates by group were published. Guselkumab is a biologic drug that blocks interleukin-23, a protein that plays a key role in inflammation in psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis, and it is given by injection under the skin. It was already approved for psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis, but proof specific to the axial form was missing, and that is what the STAR trial aims to provide. The trial protocol was published in 2022 in the journal Trials, and the trial is registered as NCT04929210, allowing researchers and doctors to follow its design and full results when published. The most important question remains: does the MRI improvement translate into tangible long-term benefit for patients, such as slowing joint damage and preserving mobility? Longer follow-up and full publication of the data will decide that.

Tip

If you have psoriatic arthritis with back pain or morning stiffness, tell your rheumatologist about it specifically: spinal pain may mean axial involvement that needs assessment and MRI imaging, and you should not assume every backache is caused by strain or posture.

What does this mean for patients?

If you have psoriatic arthritis and feel pain in your lower back or morning stiffness that improves with movement, tell your doctor: you may have the axial form of the disease without it having been diagnosed yet. In the earlier AXIS imaging study, axial involvement was found in 37.4% of patients initially, and central review of the images confirmed it in 27.4% of them, meaning more than a quarter of patients may have this involvement. The STAR results do not change your treatment today, as the drug has not yet received a new approval for this use, but they give doctors and patients hope for an additional imaging-backed treatment option. If you already take guselkumab for psoriatic arthritis, keep taking your treatment and discuss any new back pain with your doctor, and do not change your dose or injection timing on your own. Full publication of the results in a peer-reviewed journal will be the crucial next step to confirm these preliminary findings.
