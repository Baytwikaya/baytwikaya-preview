Before you exercise: the order of cardio and weights changes blood-sugar control… but not as you expect

Share this article:

Cardio first or weights first? A small study answers… with an unexpected answer

People with type 2 diabetes know that exercise helps control blood sugar, but a simple question remained without a clear answer: does the order of exercise within a single session matter? A new randomised trial published in the journal iScience in November 2026 tested this question directly: is it better to start with cardio then weights, or with weights then cardio?

The trial enrolled 56 adults with type 2 diabetes from China, with a mean age of 62.5 years, who did not exercise regularly, and randomly assigned them to two equal groups: a “cardio-first” group (28 people) and a “weights-first” group (28 people), for 8 weeks, with three sessions per week. The aerobic exercise was on a stationary bicycle for 30 minutes at 40–70% of heart-rate reserve, and the resistance exercise was on the major muscle groups at 60–80% of one-repetition maximum. The team monitored glucose with a continuous glucose monitor.

Cardio first: lower average glucose and lower night-time glucose… but higher variability

In the “cardio-first” group, mean glucose and HbA1c fell significantly over 8 weeks (mean glucose p=0.043, HbA1c p=0.007), while they did not fall in the “weights-first” group. HbA1c in the cardio-first group fell by 0.3 percentage points, a modest but real drop.

The clearest difference was at night: on rest days, night-time glucose (from midnight to 06:00) fell by 10.7% in the cardio-first group, versus a slight rise of 0.2% in the weights-first group. In week 8, glucose in the cardio-first group was lower during exercise and recovery (p=0.036), and the night-time glucose reduction was larger (p=0.015). The researchers explain this by cardio first depleting muscle glycogen stores, which pushes the body to draw glucose from the blood more strongly during the resistance exercise that follows.

But note: variability rose, and the direct difference between the groups was not decisive

Here comes the surprise that changes the picture: in the cardio-first group, the glucose variability coefficient rose from 24.1% to 26.1%, meaning glucose became less stable, while in the weights-first group it rose only from 25.1% to 25.6%, a rise that was not significant. Cardio first lowered average glucose, but it made the glucose curve more jagged — and high variability is itself a risk factor linked to diabetes complications.

And the second point that must be stated plainly: the direct difference between the two groups in HbA1c after 8 weeks was not statistically significant, and fasting glucose stayed close to baseline in both groups. Fasting insulin fell in both groups among participants not treated with insulin (cardio first p<0.001, weights first p=0.001). The precise scientific conclusion is therefore not “cardio first is better”, but “cardio first lowers the average and night-time glucose, while weights first keeps it more stable”.

So what should you choose? It depends on what matters most to you

The researchers themselves say the order should be individualised according to what the patient prioritises: if your goal is to lower HbA1c, mean glucose and night-time glucose, cardio first may suit you better; if your goal is glucose stability and fewer swings, weights first may be the better choice. In any case, exercising itself, in either order, is far better than not exercising.

It is also important to know the study’s limits: the sample is small (56 people), from a single centre in China, diet was not tracked, exercise timing was not standardised, earlier studies found no difference between the two orders, and the trial ran between November 2023 and February 2024. This is a preliminary result that deserves a larger trial before it becomes a general recommendation. And more important than the order: consistency, combining cardio and resistance in your programme, and monitoring your glucose with your doctor.

Tip

If you have diabetes and you exercise, try the order that fits your goal: cardio first if your goal is to lower mean glucose and night-time glucose, weights first if your goal is glucose stability and fewer swings. Monitor your glucose with your meter, especially at night, and discuss the result with your doctor. More important than the order: keep exercising three times a week, combine cardio and resistance, and do not stop your medicine without consulting your doctor.

The bottom line: no winner… but a trade-off between lowering glucose and stabilising it

The 2026 iScience trial in 56 people with type 2 diabetes over 8 weeks found that cardio first lowered mean glucose and HbA1c (−0.3 percentage points) and night-time glucose by 10.7%, but raised glucose variability from 24.1% to 26.1%, while weights first kept glucose more stable (25.1% to 25.6%). The direct between-group difference in HbA1c was not statistically significant, and fasting glucose stayed close to baseline in both groups. The choice depends on priority: lowering the average and night-time glucose with cardio first, or stability with weights first.
